PROPOSAL — NOT AN ESTABLISHED DISTRICT

Proposed Emergency Services District

ACFA is sharing information about a possible Emergency Services District to provide dedicated funding for fire protection and rescue services. The currently discussed area includes rural Anderson County and Elkhart and Frankston, while excluding Palestine city limits. Final boundaries would depend on the legal petition and approval process.

What the proposal is about: Funding for firefighter protective equipment, training, apparatus repairs, communications, facilities, and eventual vehicle replacement. EMS is not included in this fire-and-rescue proposal.

How a Texas ESD is created

PetitionPrepare the proposed boundaries and obtain the required qualified signatures.
Public processFile the petition, complete notice and hearing requirements, and obtain any required municipal consent.
ElectionEligible voters decide whether the proposed district will be created and authorize the applicable tax.
KNOW THE LAW

Texas Health & Safety Code — Chapter 775

Chapter 775 is the Texas law governing the creation, organization, powers, and administration of Emergency Services Districts. It explains the legal process and responsibilities of a district, including:

  • Creating a district: Petitions, hearings, elections, and municipal consent where required (Subchapter B).
  • Governance and operations: District powers, emergency services commissioners, and permissible services (Subchapter C).
  • Funding and accountability: Taxation and financial provisions applicable to ESDs.

The proposed Anderson County ESD is for fire protection and rescue, not EMS, and has not been established. Chapter 775 permits districts to provide limited services; the precise scope and boundaries would depend on the official legal process.

Understand the proposal

ESD Facts & Common Misconceptions

Straightforward answers about the proposed fire-and-rescue district. These describe a proposal, not an existing ESD.

FACT

The district has not been created

Establishment requires the applicable legal process, including a petition, public proceedings, and an election.

FACT

10¢ is a ceiling, not an automatic rate

The illustrative maximum is 10 cents per $100 of taxable value. An actual adopted rate could be lower.

FACT

ESD revenue has a defined purpose

District revenue must be used for authorized district purposes under Texas law, not unrelated county programs.

IMPORTANT

Insurance savings are not guaranteed

Better equipment, staffing and response capacity may help ISO evaluations over time, but individual insurance premiums depend on the insurer and property.

FACT

Fire and rescue—not EMS

The current Anderson County proposal focuses on fire protection and rescue. Ambulance/EMS services are not part of this proposal.

IMPORTANT

Existing department assets

Creating an ESD does not by itself establish the disposition of each VFD's property. Ownership and any future agreements would need review.

Educational summaries. Refer to the final petition and Chapter 775 for controlling terms.

Common questions

Questions about the proposed ESD

Is the ESD already established?

No. This website describes a proposal. Creating a district requires the applicable petition, public process, and voter approval.

Would everyone pay $161 per year?

No. That is only an example based on a $161,000 net taxable value and a 10¢ rate. Enter your own taxable value in the calculator to see a personalized illustration.

Is the 10¢ rate automatic?

No. Ten cents per $100 is the maximum property-tax rate for this type of ESD. A district's actual adopted rate could be lower.

Would this ESD fund ambulance services?

The proposal being discussed is for fire protection and rescue services, not EMS.

Would Palestine city limits be included?

The currently discussed proposal excludes the city limits of Palestine. Final legal boundaries must be confirmed in the official petition and related proceedings.

Could the ESD money be spent on unrelated county programs?

An ESD is a separate special-purpose governmental entity. Its revenues must be used for its lawful district purposes, subject to applicable statutes and oversight requirements.
